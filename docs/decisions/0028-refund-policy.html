ADR 0028 — Refund policy for purchased credits

Status: proposed. This is a money / consumer-law call for the owner to confirm before the Terms refund section (v6.12) is written. Not legal advice — verify the chosen stance against the consumer-protection law that applies to your users before publishing.

Context

The app sells credits via Stripe (1 credit = $0.01 CAD), as one-time purchases — no subscription. Credits are consumable digital goods: each scan debits the ledger. Taking real money creates a consumer-law expectation of a clearly stated refund policy, and the Terms page (v6.12) needs concrete wording for its refund section.

Relevant existing mechanic: the credit ledger already models kind='refund' entries, so a scan that fails after a debit can be auto-credited back (the user is not charged for a scan that did not produce a result). That is an operational auto-credit, distinct from a commercial refund of a purchase to the card.

Decision (proposed)

Lean toward Option A below, pending owner confirmation:

  • Purchased credits are non-refundable once bought, except where applicable consumer law requires otherwise.
  • Failed scans are not charged — if a scan errors after debiting, the credit is automatically returned to the balance (the existing ledger refund mechanic). This is stated so users understand they only pay for results.
  • Discretionary goodwill — the operator may refund an unused balance on request, at its discretion, but does not guarantee it.

Options considered

  • A — Non-refundable + failed-scan auto-credit (proposed). Standard for consumable digital credits; pairs with the ledger refund mechanic; minimal ongoing operational burden.
  • B — Time-boxed window on unused balance (e.g. refund the unspent portion within N days of purchase). More consumer-friendly; needs a small refund path (Stripe refund + ledger reversal) and a rule for partially-spent purchases.
  • C — Discretionary case-by-case, no stated rule. Maximum flexibility but weakest from a consumer-law / trust standpoint; "no clear policy" is itself a red flag for a paid product.

Consequences

  • + A is clear, conventional, and cheap to operate; the only mandatory money-back path (failed scans) is already built.
  • − Cross-border consumer law may override "non- refundable": e.g. EU/UK statutory withdrawal rights for digital content can apply unless the buyer expressly consents to immediate performance and waives them at purchase. If you have such users, the checkout copy and Terms must reflect that — A's "except where law requires" clause is the hook, but confirm the specifics.
  • − If B is later required, it adds a Stripe-refund + ledger-reversal flow not currently built.
  • The accepted wording is transcribed into the Terms refund section (v6.12 copy drop) and, if relevant, surfaced at checkout.

Needs owner confirmation

  • Pick A, B, or C (default proposed: A).
  • Confirm where your users are, to know which consumer-protection regime(s) apply (PIPEDA/Canada at minimum; EU/UK/other?).
  • Whether to add an explicit "immediate performance / waiver" consent at checkout if statutory withdrawal rights are in play.
