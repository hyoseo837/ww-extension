ADR 0050 — Friend-invitation bonus: one-sided 20 credits on the invitee's first scan, email attribution, ADR 0029 suppression shared

Context

The September wave (ADR 0042) needs word-of-mouth, and the product's audience — co-op cohorts hitting the same WaterlooWorks deadlines in the same weeks — is a dense referral network. A friend-invitation bonus converts "this is neat" into "sign up with my email". The idea matured on the whiteboard and the owner confirmed the product calls on 2026-06-11.

Referral programs usually die by self-invite farming. Here the fraud surface is unusually small: sign-in is UWaterloo Microsoft auth (ADR 0036) — one identity per student — and ADR 0029 already retains an email-hash suppression record across account deletion, so delete-and-re-register cannot mint fresh referrable identities.

Decision

  • One-sided: the inviter gets the bonus, the invitee gets nothing extra. Owner's call — people who find the service themselves are just as precious, and the invitee already receives the signup bonus. One-sided also keeps the incentive aimed at the person doing the inviting.
  • 20 credits per activated referral (≈ 10 scans at the ~2 credits/scan rate, ADR 0048). New ledger kind referral_bonus, granted to the inviter with ref = the invitee's user id.
  • Activation trigger, not signup trigger. The bonus is granted when the invitee's first successful scan settles — rewarding a real activated user, not an account row. Mechanically: every successful scan settlement attempts an insert-or-ignore of the bonus; a partial unique index (ref where kind='referral_bonus') makes it at most once per invitee, so no "is this the first scan?" bookkeeping exists at all.
  • Attribution by email, asked once at onboarding — no referral links or codes. The preferences-setup wizard (ADR 0040) gains an optional "Who invited you?" step: the invitee types the inviter's UWaterloo email. Free text beats link/code infrastructure: nothing to generate, track, or leak. Attribution is rejected when the email is the invitee's own, matches no account, an attribution already exists, or the invitee has already scanned (the question is a signup-time question; answering after activating would make the grant retroactive).
  • Suppression shares ADR 0029's mechanism. An account whose email hash sits in signup_bonus_block (i.e. a deleted-and-re-registered identity) is not referral-eligible: it can neither be attributed nor re-earn anyone a bonus. This closes the pair-farming loop (A invites B → B scans → B deletes → B re-registers and attributes A again).

Consequences

  • + A referral channel exists before the September wave, at near-zero marginal cost (20 credits ≈ $0.20 nominal, far less in actual Gemini spend).
  • + Farming requires distinct UW identities and a real scan per bonus — the strongest practical deterrent available without building verification machinery.
  • − Attribution stores a who-invited-whom link (PII linkage). It is backend-only, cascades away on either account's deletion, joins the account export, and gets a line in the privacy policy.
  • − The attribution endpoint confirms whether an email has an account (a 404 on no match). Accepted: within a closed UW cohort this is low-risk, and the honest error means a typo'd email is fixable instead of silently costing the inviter the bonus.
  • If the invitee deletes their account before their first scan, the attribution cascades away and no bonus is ever granted — correct by construction.

Alternatives considered

  • Two-sided bonus ("you both get N") — slightly more viral, but rejected by the owner: self-found users shouldn't be worth less than invited ones, and one-sided is simpler.
  • Referral links/codes — standard, but pure infrastructure: generation, attribution windows, link leakage to strangers. Email entry inside a UW-only cohort gets the same attribution with one text field.
  • Grant on signup instead of first scan — rejected: pays for account rows, invites farming, and misaligns the incentive (an inviter should want their friend to actually try it).
