ADR 0042 — Project direction: portfolio flagship; v8 is the final major for now; effort shifts to marketing

Context

With v8.5.7 launched (2026-06-10) the product is complete and live: extension on the Web Store, web app deployed, backend serving accounts and credits, required onboarding wizard, rebuilt preferences, hardened scoring prompt. What it does not have is real users. The build-up since v1 always had an obvious next step; for the first time the next step is a strategic choice between (1) running it as a niche micro-business for UWaterloo co-op students, (2) treating it as a portfolio flagship whose primary payoff is the owner's career, or (3) generalizing beyond WaterlooWorks.

Decision

  • Direction 2 — portfolio flagship — is the floor. The product's primary value is as a complete, shipped, documented, revenue-capable artifact. Engineering quality and documentation discipline continue to matter for that reason.
  • v8 is the final major version for now. No v9 is planned. Feature development is frozen; remaining work is small polish (the open whiteboard fixes) and anything required to keep the deployed product healthy. The vX.Y.Z scheme continues unchanged for whatever maintenance ships.
  • Effort shifts from code to marketing and user acquisition — outside the codebase: visibility among UWaterloo co-op students, with the September 2026 recruiting wave (Fall applications for Winter 2027) as the natural distribution moment.
  • The business question gets one fair test. Revisit direction at the end of Fall term 2026 (December) with real adoption data. Meaningful organic use → reopen feature work (a v9) around growth; otherwise the project stays a maintained portfolio piece. The whiteboard's subscription / term-pass question is explicitly deferred to that December review.

Consequences

  • Parked feature ideas (feedback loop, cover letters, re-score on demand, Employer-Student-Direct page reach) stay on the whiteboard / roadmap "Further out" — none are scheduled. The feedback loop is the first candidate to un-park if marketing produces users worth listening to.
  • Maintenance commits still follow the phase rules (spec for anything non-trivial, version bumps, dev → master releases per ADR 0039).
  • The scraping-fragility risk (WaterlooWorks DOM, no official blessing) is accepted as-is — it caps the business upside but does not threaten the portfolio value.
  • Marketing artifacts (write-ups, demo media, store-listing copy) are owner-driven; the repo documents the product, not the campaign.

Alternatives considered

  • Niche micro-business now — premature with zero users; the December checkpoint keeps this open as an earned upgrade rather than a bet.
  • Generalize beyond WaterlooWorks — rejected: trades the only structural advantage (deep WW integration, a niche the owner inhabits) for a crowded market.
  • Keep building features (open v9) — rejected for now: features don't fix a zero-user product; distribution might.
