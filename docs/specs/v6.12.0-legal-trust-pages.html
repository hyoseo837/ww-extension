v6.12.0 — Legal & trust pages

Goal

Finish the legal, trust & support work the roadmap opened as the v6.10 phase. Its first slice — the support address — already shipped as v6.10.0. The remaining slices ship here: in-app /privacy and /terms pages, a stated refund policy, the "not affiliated with UW / WaterlooWorks" footer disclaimer, and a consent-at-signup line.

Why v6.12 and not v6.10.x: versioning is monotonic and matches manifest.json (ADR 0002). v6.11 shipped ahead of this work, so HEAD is already 6.11.2 — the legal pages take the next free minor, v6.12.0. Roadmap data-export shifts to v6.13 and polish to v6.14 accordingly.

Inputs & constraints

  • Legal copy is owner-supplied. The owner provides the final binding Privacy + Terms text (via a reputable policy generator or legal review). Until then the pages render the scaffold-derived outline behind a visible DRAFT banner. Source of structure + known facts: docs/reference/legal-pages-scaffold.html.
  • Refund policy is an open decision — see ADR 0028 (status: proposed). Real money via Stripe carries a consumer-law expectation of a clear policy. The Terms "refund" section is blocked on that ADR being accepted.
  • Owner-supplied placeholders the copy needs: operator/legal name, governing law (e.g. Ontario, Canada), age/eligibility, effective date. Tracked as [FILL] in the scaffold.

Routing change (the one real technical decision)

Today App.tsx does if (!session) return <Landing /> — there is no router at all for logged-out visitors, so a public /privacy URL can't resolve. The legal pages must be reachable both logged out (footer + consent links on the landing) and logged in. Restructure so the legal routes are public:

  • Logged out: render a small <Routes> — /privacy → Privacy, /terms → Terms, and * → Landing (so the landing stays the default and any other path falls back to it, preserving today's behaviour).
  • Logged in: add /privacy and /terms alongside the existing authed routes, rendered in a plain public layout (not the app sidebar) so the page reads the same in both states.

The pages themselves are auth-agnostic static content, so a single LegalPage/LegalLayout wrapper renders them identically regardless of session.
